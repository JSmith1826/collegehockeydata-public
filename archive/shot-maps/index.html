How locations are flagged

Behind-line locations fall strictly behind the source rink’s goal line. Frequent locations have at least three shown attempts at the same recorded coordinate; up to five per category are marked on each map. Ties favor more mapped games, then coordinate order. These are descriptive flags, not statistical outliers. The source’s rounded coordinates can make different real locations look identical. Shares use all shown attempts, including origins beyond this half-rink. League-difference colors do not change these counting rules.

Coverage & how to read these maps

Recorded locations.

Locations come from College Hockey News. Maps combine a season’s recorded attempts. Goals are included in shots on net; missed attempts include posts where the source groups them together. Blocked dots represent source-recorded attempt locations, without puck paths.

Shot timestamps and strength labels are unavailable. Scorer names are linked only for unambiguous scoring-table matches, with attribution coverage shown beside each list. Every view combines game states. These maps describe patterns rather than predict scoring or assign expected goals.

A consistent view of the ice.

Both maps point toward the same attacking end using CHN’s display convention. Left and right describe the displayed map. The archive’s original coordinate scale is retained; distances in feet are not estimated.

Heatmaps use a fixed smoothing kernel. Colors are linear relative to the largest smoothed value across both maps; the scale updates with the filters. Contours trace equal concentration. They describe where attempts gather, not the probability of a goal. Optional goal markers show only goals included by the outcome filter.

The home plate opens from the crease edges to the faceoff dots and reaches the circle edge toward the blue line. A subtle net-front arc defines S1; a broad, gently flattened dot-to-dot arch separates S2 from S3. S3 is one connected outer-slot band completing the home plate to the tops of the circles, with both shoulders joined across the center. The curve stays fixed across teams and filters. Each side outside the home plate has a broad low wing for tight-angle ice around the goal line and an upper wing toward the point. One horizontal split divides them, halfway between the near edge and center of the faceoff circle. Each low wing has a straight rear edge from its crease-edge scoring origin to the boards, angled 5 degrees behind the goal line in the displayed geometry. Ice beyond those lines and directly behind the net is Behind the net. Center point is a narrow central lane between wider left and right point areas. The point starts at the tops of the circles toward the blue line. Origins beyond CHN?s drawn blue line remain one area. The source display has not been independently calibrated to physical rink markings or game video; this classification should not be read as a verified count of shots from neutral ice. Hover, keyboard focus or tap updates the fixed panels beneath both rinks; the last inspected area stays visible. Every attempt is counted once. Scorer links require the archived scoring table and chart to agree for both teams in every period. A name is assigned only when a team-period group has one scorer identity; multi-scorer groups remain unlinked. Neither dot order nor scoring-table row order supplies a match. These boundaries do not establish a validated shot-danger model.

Count the coverage.

Each team's locations are checked separately. By default, goals must match and shots on net may differ by one; these samples are flagged. All source dots remain unchanged. Larger differences and goal disagreements need review. Choose Exact matches only for strict analysis. Missing charts and unknown directions are excluded. Goal/on-net agreement does not prove complete miss/block reporting.

Compare like with like.

Share uses the shown outcome’s total, including origins outside this half-rink. Attempts per team game uses mapped team appearances, including games with zero of the selected outcome. A conference game involving two selected programs counts as two team appearances.

League comparisons use all teams in the selected season with the same opponent, period, venue, scope, and coverage filters. They show percentage-point differences in location share. Venue is the source’s home/away designation, including neutral-site games. Postseason includes named conference tournaments and the NCAA tournament.
