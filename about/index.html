01 / THE BACKSTORY

I’ve always
kept the numbers.

Hockey fan. Former player.
Michigan State supporter.
Stat head from the start.

I’m a lifelong hockey guy, a long-ago player, and a Michigan State fan. I’ve always been a stat head. I was the kid keeping spray charts of my Little League at-bats and learning how to use a spreadsheet to track my slugging percentage.

After years as an Excel and Access jockey, It was time to learn some new tools. Data visualization was a particular interest.

I worked through courses and exercises, watched too many Jon Bois videos, and had a thought: if I was going to put time into learning, I should do it with data I cared about.

I started with baseball—a shout-out here to Learn to Code with Baseball. But plenty of people were already doing baseball. I wanted to work on something that might be getting overlooked.

Meanwhile, Michigan State hockey was on the rise under Adam Nightingale. That was the light-bulb moment. College hockey gave me a sport I loved, questions I wanted to answer, and a reason to keep learning.

03 / OPENING THE DOORS

From my charts
to your questions.

This site was originally an internal tool: a way to set parameters, choose a team, and output a chart without having to work through the code every time.

Before long, I realized that other people should be able to play around with the tools themselves. A chart can answer the question I had in mind. An interactive study lets everyone ask their own.

THE SITE WENT LIVESeptember 30, 2026

Just in time for the 2026–27 season.

It was a rush to get everything online before the season started, but here it is, rough around the edges. The project keeps growing, including more tools and data for women’s hockey, and I’m continuously looking for ways to improve the visualizations and the insights they offer.

If you have a question, spot something that needs correcting, or have an idea for a study, I’d love to hear it. Leave a request or comment on the Contact page.

CHD IMAGE REUSE TERMS · OCTOBER 13, 2026

You’re welcome to share CHD chart exports for personal or commercial use—in articles, social posts, videos, presentations, and broadcasts. No fee or individual permission is needed when you follow these terms.

Keep the credits in the image. The CollegeHockeyData.com label and the data-source credits must remain visible and legible. Do not remove, cover, or replace them.

Resizing and cropping are fine. Apart from those changes and file-format conversion, keep the chart content unchanged. Preserve the season, filters, scales, and labels needed to understand the portion you share; a crop should not change what the data appears to say.

Give readers a way back. Credit College Hockey Data and link to the original study wherever your medium supports links. A caption, video description, companion article, or production credits is a good place for that link. In print or on screen, retain the visible website label.
